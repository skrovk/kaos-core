# Common graph operation design principles

Synthesis of the agreed [implementation answers](graph_operation_design_questions.md). Provisional choices, accepted limitations and unanswered details retain their status in that decision record.

1. **Choose the smallest sufficient mechanism.** Build for the research experiments and six-operation baseline. Reuse existing identities, removal operations and standard transport; qualify WAMR before considering replacement. Add abstractions, metadata or stronger services only when a concrete requirement justifies their cost. Validate the baseline before extending it. ([Q03](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q03), [Q08–Q10](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q08), [Q17–Q19](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q17), [Q22–Q23](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q22))

2. **Budget and own the entire lifecycle.** Admission must cover peak preparation, execution, reporting and cleanup, including partial failures. Give each allocation one cleanup owner; track accesses until reclamation is safe. Protect live state and unfinished obligations while reclaiming eligible history. Defer admissions when protected capacity is exhausted; report CLEANED only after full resource release. ([Q01](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q01), [Q06–Q07](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q06), [Q12–Q13](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q12), [Q16](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q16))

3. **Localize responsibility and preserve independent progress.** The orchestrator owns authorization and complete intended scope; each device owns admission, resources and closure. Serialize short conflicting decisions, then let independent work proceed concurrently. Keep control, deadlines and cleanup service available under load. Existing applications and admitted work continue within their constraints when management or archival storage is unavailable. ([Q02](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q02), [Q04](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q04), [Q06](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q06), [Q14–Q15](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q14))

4. **Preserve precise meanings across boundaries.** Bind commands, callbacks and evidence to exact identities, sources and lifetimes. Distinguish receipt, admission, execution, closure and evidence storage; distinguish historical success from current health and local observation time from acquisition age. Missing evidence remains uncertainty. Queries and retries preserve authority and deadlines; new intent requires a new authorized operation. ([Q03–Q04](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q03), [Q07](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q07), [Q12](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q12), [Q15](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q15), [Q17–Q20](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q17))

5. **Validate the guarantee actually claimed.** Match each claim to suitable evidence: TLA+ for modeled safety/liveness, runtime tests for lifecycle behavior, ESP32 measurements for embedded feasibility, and observed control behavior for Quality of Control. Keep assumptions and accepted gaps explicit. Stronger reference protocols, simulator knowledge and available APIs do not establish guarantees the implementation has not demonstrated. ([Q01](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q01), [Q05](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q05), [Q08](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q08), [Q14](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q14), [Q20–Q23](graph_operation_design_questions.md#q20))
