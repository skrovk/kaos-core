# Graph operation implementation questions

Terminology: [shared glossary](graph_operation_glossary.md).

Prepared on 1 October 2026, before implementation changes, and revised to incorporate the user's permission to replace existing feature implementations completely. Existing APIs, wire formats, guest entrypoints, storage layouts, task structure, libraries and build integration may change. Backward compatibility with Beacon LOAD, current binaries or internal interfaces is not a requirement of this plan; a compatibility adapter is not a default work item. The documented protocol behavior, WebAssembly application model, embedded constraints and evaluation in `sim_env` remain the design inputs.

The questions below concern the replacement design and remain open unless an answer is recorded explicitly. Suggested starting points are proposals, not decisions inferred from silence. The companion [implementation plan](graph_operation_implementation_plan.md) and [test catalogue](graph_operation_test_plan.md) can be reviewed without choosing every parameter now. Existing code supplies examples and hazards to consider, rather than constraints on the new architecture.

The baseline is the [shared mechanisms](../../../Thesis/protocol%20specs/graph_operation_shared_mechanisms.md), [node addition v2](../../../Thesis/protocol%20specs/node_addition_protocol_v2.md), five derived protocols, and [implementation notes](../../../Thesis/protocol%20specs/graph_operation_implementation_notes.md). Questions concern their concrete realization or an explicitly identified extension. Receiver-first activation, full cleanup scope, immutable requests, historical success, and bounded admission are already specified; they are not open choices.

### Q01

**Status: planning answer agreed; numerical qualification pending. The user explicitly requested moving on to Q02.**

Agreed on 1 October 2026:

- The primary supported hardware profile is the user's original ESP32-based [LILYGO TTGO T-Display](https://github.com/Xinyuan-LilyGO/TTGO-T-Display). Assume no PSRAM; baseline feasibility must fit without external RAM.
- Wi-Fi is the only required wireless connectivity. Bluetooth support and Wi-Fi/Bluetooth coexistence are outside the required baseline. The protocol transport over Wi-Fi remains a Q08 decision.
- The initial minimum workload is two resident WebAssembly nodes on one board, connected by one local channel. This is a minimum feasibility target, not an upper limit on supported nodes or channels. The channel has two distinct local endpoint roles, even if its implementation shares buffer storage.
- Physical-board measurements will establish feasibility as part of the project's evaluation. ESP32 emulator tests may supplement baseline testing; no emulator has been selected and emulator support is not a committed work item.
- Numerical resource budgets and supported upper limits will be derived during an early feasibility prototype, with the two-node/one-channel topology as the required minimum. Enforce finite configurable bounds from the first prototype; record guest memory requirements, payload sizes and traffic rates as explicit experiment parameters. Account for peak preparation/cleanup overlap, retained evidence, and control/cleanup headroom when qualifying a profile.
- A Raspberry Pi Pico board is a potential future extension. No particular Pico model or commitment to deliver that port is established.

Hardware reference: the selected board's [vendor instructions](https://github.com/Xinyuan-LilyGO/TTGO-T-Display#quick-start) specify disabled PSRAM and a 4 MB flash configuration. This is nominal hardware capacity, not a usable firmware/artifact budget. Record the actual board revision, flash capacity and firmware configuration with measurements.

Prototype deliverables: usable RAM/flash budgets with Wi-Fi enabled; supported limits for resident instances, incident channels/local endpoint roles, concurrent operations, payload/buffer sizes and retained results; explicit guest memory and traffic parameters; and measured headroom through addition, operation and removal. Initial numerical settings are provisional until measured. This decision establishes the target and sizing method, not a claim of measured feasibility.

Rationale: an early physical-board prototype can expose resource constraints before storage layouts and runtime integration are fixed; the full project evaluation then measures the completed implementation. The minimum topology exercises co-located runtimes and both local endpoint roles. Capacity must also cover lifecycle work and retained obligations while application resources are occupied.

Evaluation boundary: a local channel does not exercise Wi-Fi endpoint traffic; distributed channel feasibility needs a separate placement variant. Emulator results support functional checks only within the chosen emulator's documented fidelity; they do not by themselves establish physical-board memory or wireless timing feasibility.

### Q02

**Status: agreed. The user explicitly requested moving on to Q03.**

Agreed on 1 October 2026: crash-stop is sufficient for the first implementation and project evaluation, for both KaOS devices and the orchestrator O. Reboot recovery, rejoining an existing deployment, and coordinator takeover are deferred to a separately specified extension.

This preserves the current protocol baseline: [V2 A7 and Section 6.6](../../../Thesis/protocol%20specs/node_addition_protocol_v2.md) and [shared assumptions/M7](../../../Thesis/protocol%20specs/graph_operation_shared_mechanisms.md) exclude restart and takeover.

Temporary loss of connectivity with protocol state retained remains in scope. A live O can reconnect and query retained results by the original operation identities; this does not establish recovery of an O process that has lost its authorization and reservation records. Already accepted work continues independently of O's availability under its existing guards and deadlines.

Reaffirmed during Q06 on 2 October 2026: decentralization includes devices continuing existing applications and already-authorized work when O loses its state. Reconstructing O's authority and resuming management after that loss remains a future extension. Q06's pressure-eviction policy can limit which historical reports are still retrievable after an outage.

The user subsequently requested keeping a mechanism to rebuild O's system view after state loss as a future design consideration. [Q06](#q06) records this follow-up separately from restoring O's authority to issue operations; neither is a committed baseline recovery feature.

Consequences of the decision:

- Missing closure evidence after an owner crash remains an obligation. A live coordinator can report Unresolved at its cutoff; a crashed coordinator can leave O without an authoritative outcome. Neither silence nor physical reboot proves protocol closure.
- Rebooted participants do not automatically resume or rejoin the existing live system. A separate laboratory experiment requires isolation from the previous run's traffic and authority; resetting one participant's counters alone is insufficient. This test-harness boundary is separate from an orchestrator deployment workflow made up of graph operations. Surviving participants may continue whatever work remains admissible without the crashed participant.
- Recovery would need separate rules for retained or reconstructed identities, authorization/reservations, removal/replay protection and outstanding outcomes, plus exclusion of old authority and traffic. Restoring guest state and repeating application effects are further choices, not consequences of rebooting the process.

Rationale and accepted limitation: this preserves the specified and modeled fault scope and keeps recovery storage outside the initial embedded budget, but it permits stranded resources and permanently unresolved operations after a crash. The first implementation will be evaluated for safe handling of failures within that scope; automatic service restoration after reboot is outside its claims.

### Q03

**Status: agreed. The user authorized moving on once its outstanding points were resolved, and has now agreed to the final two choices. Discussion moves to Q04.**

Agreed on 1 October 2026:

- O is the sole allocator of protocol identities.
- Use one unsigned 64-bit `opId` field. Its value is allocated by O's counter; no separate operation-sequence or deployment-ID field is required. The user concluded that 64 bits are sufficient after discussing a system lifetime of up to 50 years and an estimated safe allocation rate of 10 new IDs per second. These are sizing assumptions, not a measured or enforced rate limit.
- Each concrete node instance has one immutable unsigned 64-bit instance ID, equal to the numeric value of its creating node-addition operation's `opId`. Keep operation IDs and instance IDs distinct in the API. Every supported node addition creates exactly one instance; its identity remains valid after the detailed addition result is retired. Membership in several services does not give that runtime several protocol identities.
- Each channel binding retains the ID of the operation that created it. The pair `(channelId, creatingOpId)` identifies that particular binding for subsequent commands, including removal. A later request has its own `opId` and separately identifies the binding it targets. This allocates no additional reference ID or namespace.
- Additional pipeline/group IDs and a mandatory service/role membership table are deferred. The baseline uses explicit channels and contracts, retaining the existing channel `serviceId`; O maintains the graph as operations change it. Future grouping can refer to existing instance identities without renaming them.
- A deployment or redeployment expressed as a list of graph operations follows the ordinary rules for those operations. Grouping operations in O does not discharge cleanup obligations or establish an identity-reuse boundary.
- Counter values are never recycled within the system lifetime. Check for exhaustion before arithmetic can wrap, and return an explicit error for requests needing a fresh ID. Existing authorized work, duplicates and reports continue with their existing identities. No live rollover protocol is required for the baseline.

For example, node addition 41 creates instance 41; node removal 57 targets instance 41. The shared numeric value at creation avoids a second allocator without conflating a later request with its target.

Protocol requirements: O records an immutable request before sending it; commands and evidence identify the operation and exact affected instance/binding. Operation and instance identities are not reused within the modeled execution. A later removal has its own operation identity and still targets the original creation. See [shared M1/M2/M6](../../../Thesis/protocol%20specs/graph_operation_shared_mechanisms.md) and [implementation notes](../../../Thesis/protocol%20specs/graph_operation_implementation_notes.md).

Rationale for one instance identity: the considered semantic tuple `(serviceId, nodeIdWithinService, physicalDeviceId)` can repeat after removal and later addition on the same device. A no-simultaneous-copies restriction does not distinguish those creations. Multiple semantic names would also need a canonical mapping so admission, resource accounting, evidence and full-incidence removal all refer to the same runtime. A later creation needs a fresh instance identity, and accepted commands retain their concrete target rather than resolving a mutable semantic name again. The suggested no-copy-on-one-device restriction has not been adopted as a placement policy or an identity requirement.

Grouping rationale: the user observed that service/role membership records could duplicate relationships already described by manifest channels. One CO2 source feeding both occupancy and ventilation is the motivating shared-node example. None of the six current operations requires a separate application-group boundary, so that additional identity is deferred.

Agreed baseline: use the declared channels and their contracts as the initial graph representation, without requiring an additional pipeline ID or service/role membership table. Retain the protocol's existing channel `serviceId`. O can assemble a graph spanning many hops from these direct relationships; devices need the instance/channel information required for their local ownership and operation scope. Omitting a pipeline ID does not limit O to analyzing immediate neighbours.

Conditions and limits of this decision:

- O's authorized graph and current observations incorporate later edge additions/removals and outstanding operations. Original node-addition manifests alone cannot establish complete current incidence; authorized, pending and unresolved bindings remain relevant, as required by M1/M6. Authorization and observed activation/closure are distinct facts.
- Graph traversal identifies upstream/downstream relationships and potential impact. Required/optional channel contracts and startup predicates add information, but do not by themselves prove ongoing application service or end-to-end health.
- Topology does not uniquely determine semantic application boundaries when branches share nodes or form cycles. A future pipeline/application grouping can express independent lifecycle, ownership or service objectives if those become requirements, with shared membership explicitly defined.
- V2 Section 2 permits the same channel `serviceId` on disjoint node pairs. It can label related channels, but its presence alone does not establish a connected, complete or uniquely delimited pipeline. An eventual group identifier must have explicitly defined semantics relative to that existing field.
- Future grouping can refer to existing immutable instance IDs without renaming them. Such metadata would not automatically add atomic group operations, migration or recovery to the six-operation protocol.

Additional grouping can be reconsidered when an operation or evaluation requirement needs those semantics.

Correction after review: withdraw the proposed 128-bit deployment UUID. It conflated an isolated execution/test boundary with an orchestrator deployment workflow, and its cost was not justified for the agreed single-O, crash-stop baseline. A deployment workflow neither resets an allocator nor proves that old traffic has disappeared. A new namespace would also not prove closure of old objects. No replacement namespace or epoch mechanism has been selected.

The Q03 identity and exhaustion decisions are agreed. Concrete encoding, internal handle lifetime and obsolete-command rejection remain with their respective later questions.

Sizing calculation using the user's estimates: at 10 new IDs per second for 50 years (365.25 days/year), O allocates 15,778,800,000 IDs. An unsigned 32-bit integer has a maximum value of 4,294,967,295 and, starting at 1 without reuse, is exhausted after about 13.6 years at that rate. The 50-year envelope needs at least 34 bits; the selected 64-bit scalar occupies 8 bytes and provides substantial headroom. This counts fresh allocations across O's entire system; retransmissions retaining an existing ID do not consume new values. It is independent of how few operation records are retained concurrently.

Deadline clarification: the user suggested that an operation deadline might make a smaller, reusable ID space sufficient. Under M7, an addition deadline bounds the wait for successful addition, while a cleanup cutoff can return Unresolved and still admit later CLEANED evidence. Neither deadline establishes that old messages/callbacks have disappeared or that an unreachable owner has released its objects. A successful addition can create a node or channel that remains live long after that operation finishes; its identity must remain valid for that object's lifetime. A smaller reusable ID space would therefore need an additional proven reuse mechanism or stronger lifetime assumptions.

Clarification of the agreed binding reference: distinguish identifying the command from selecting its target. For example, a node-removal request has its own `opId` and an existing `targetInstanceId`. A channel-removal request likewise has its own `opId`, plus information identifying the exact channel binding it targets. The pair `(channelId, creatingOpId)` uses existing identifying values; it creates no new namespace and requires no separately allocated reference ID or pointer.

Exhaustion/reuse rationale: silent arithmetic wrap is unsafe if old commands, callbacks, data or live object identities can still match a recycled value. Question: how should a receiver identify superseded evidence for the same object, while handling retries, report acknowledgements and local callbacks correctly?

Initial motivating example: Ki records endpoint E as READY and emits revision 5, then records failure and emits revision 6. User challenge and review response, 2 October 2026:

- The user asks whether reliable transport for the infrastructure would make revisions unnecessary. It can, for an end-to-end ordered reporting path. Reliable delivery alone does not specify message ordering; [TCP's documented service](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9293.html#section-2.2) provides a reliable, in-order byte stream on a connection. If READY is written before failure to that connection and the receiver processes the framed reports in that order, network packet delay/retransmission cannot reverse them. The initial example therefore does not justify an extra revision field under those conditions.

The physical dynamics have only two continuous states: room concentration `C` (ppm) and actual mechanical outdoor airflow `q` (m³/s). Occupancy `N(t)` is a scripted input. The constant-density reduction of the cited CO2 balance is:

```text
dC/dt = 10^6 * N(t) * g / V + (q + q_background) * (C_outdoor - C) / V
dq/dt = (q_target(u) - q) / tau
q_target(u) = q_max * clamp(u, 0, 1)
```

Here `V > 0` is room volume (m³), `g >= 0` is pure CO2 generation (m³/s/person at the same reference conditions), `q_background >= 0` is constant background outdoor-air exchange (m³/s), `q_max >= 0` is maximum mechanical outdoor airflow, `tau > 0` is actuator response time (s), and time is in seconds. Initialize `q` within its physical bounds. Explicit power-loss scenarios override `q_target` to zero; natural airflow decay follows the configured lag. Numerical integration must preserve physical bounds and pass step-size qualification. Do not freeze the plant when a guest stops or accelerate it independently of controller/network/deadline time without a separately qualified time mapping.

Exclude temperature, humidity, thermal comfort, detailed energy consumption, pressure/wind, spatial concentration differences and interactions with other rooms. Consequently this model supports evaluation of CO2-based control quality, airflow tracking and disturbance/failure response under the chosen assumptions; it cannot support thermal-comfort, energy-efficiency or location-specific exposure claims. Keep the supervisory policy, local feedback algorithm, validation, network and KaOS scheduling outside the physical equations. Plant traces expose truth to the evaluator; guests use their simulated peripheral observations. Numerical parameters, the Q20 QoC criterion, and application fallback remain open.
